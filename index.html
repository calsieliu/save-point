01 · MISSION BRIEFING

What is Save Point?

A four-week city game in Paris for Gen Z. Every Saturday a new district opens as a level. Players solve clues in local shops, save their progress at a photo booth, and unlock a final stage. It should feel like playing a game, not attending a brand campaign.

In 2028, Gen Z is buried in chaos and algorithms.

They still want to go out. They need a reason that is worth it, and people to share it with.

Save Point gives them a goal for every Saturday: clear the level, save the game, find your team.

02 · HOW TO PLAY

One player, six steps

Follow a test player through the whole game. Click a step, or press Play to run them in order.

STEP 01 / 06

Press Start

Peter sees a strange screen on a metro billboard that says “PRESS START”. He scans the QR code.

Trend link: Shoppable touchpoint. The billboard is the game’s front door.

STEP 02 / 06

Pick a mood

He answers one question: “How do you feel today?” His answer gives him a character card. The color of the card’s border shows his team.

Trend link: Mood-based personalisation and fragmented identities.

STEP 03 / 06

Enter the level

On Saturday he goes to the district that opened this week. He solves clues with shop owners. The clues are props made by local artists.

Trend link: IRL gaming and human premium.

STEP 04 / 06

Save the game

At the end of the level he “saves” at the photo booth. He takes home a print of his character card photo with that week’s badge.

Trend link: #BadTaste meme photo booths. The reward is something to share.

STEP 05 / 06

Unlock the final

After three saves he gets clues during decode week. He has to work out where the final stage will happen.

Trend link: Selective engagement. The goal makes every Saturday count.

STEP 06 / 06

Keep playing

After the finale he can buy the city board game and keep playing at home. He may also find his photo in the “Player Hall” on Instagram.

Trend link: Story extension and cross-platform IP.

TEST PLAYER · USER SIMULATION

Peter, 23, 3D animator

Born in 2005. Works fully remote and has lived in Paris for five years. Online for more than ten hours a day. Loves memes, retro games, board games and Y2K photo booths. Hates brand ads, but joins anything that is fun enough to feel like a game.

Most of his friends are online, so real life feels a bit lonely. He is tired of algorithm picks and wants a reason to go out with real people.

06 · WHY 2028

Know the world

before you play it

The idea comes from WGSN trend forecasts for 2028. Below are the trends, the data and the player problems that shaped each game mechanic.

Gen Z 2028: too much chaos, too many algorithms, not enough reasons to go out.

  • Turning games into real places already works for nostalgia and sharing. A real city game goes further and builds real connection.
  • People go out more in summer. Salomon’s outdoor, technical shoes fit a game played on foot across the city.
  • Local shops and artists take part, which gives the game a human, handmade feel.

Six trend stats

Connecting in Chaos

T1

AI makes life faster and noisier. Brands that create short moments of calm will matter.

Fragmented Identities

T1

People connect through many small, specific groups instead of one big one.

Joyful Audacity

T2

Brands stop selling happiness. They let people take part and create.

Snark

T2

Absurd, anti-aesthetic humor pushes back against polished perfection.

Selective Engagement

T2

People only join what is fun and different. Play and experience come first.

Human Premium

T3

In the next stage of AI, content that people clearly made with care stands out to Gen Z.
